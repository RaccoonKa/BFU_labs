# Mathematical and Computer Modeling

An educational course covering the fundamentals of mathematical modeling and computational methods for simulating real-world systems. The materials include lab assignments on conservation laws, dynamical systems, numerical methods, and stochastic modeling.

## What the course is about

The course is built around the practical implementation of mathematical models and numerical algorithms to solve physical and computational problems.

**Conservation laws and collisions.** Students build mathematical models of partially elastic collisions, analyze energy and momentum conservation, and visualize energy distribution before and after impact.

**Dynamical systems.** Lab assignments cover bifurcation points, equilibrium stability in continuous and discrete systems, phase portraits, and the analysis of ordinary differential equations and maps.

**Numerical methods.** The course explores difference schemes, approximation errors, stability conditions, and the construction of stencils for solving differential equations.

**Stochastic and fractal models.** Students implement the Monte-Carlo method, analyze random walks on 2D lattices, build attractors from iterated function systems, and estimate metric dimension.

**Visualization.** A significant part of the course is dedicated to plotting phase portraits, energy graphs, and high-quality visual representations of numerical results to build intuition.

**Outcome.** The course provides hands-on experience with mathematical modeling, from formulating conservation laws to implementing numerical schemes and interpreting the results.
