# Discrete Mathematics

An educational course covering the foundations of discrete mathematics that underpin computer science. The materials include lab assignments on logic, set theory, graph theory, and combinatorics.

## What the course is about

The course is built around a practical understanding of discrete structures and how they are used to model and solve problems in computing.

**Mathematical logic.** Students study propositional and predicate logic, truth tables, logical equivalences, and methods of proof, building the foundation for formal reasoning in programming.

**Set theory and relations.** Lab assignments cover sets, operations on sets, relations, functions, and equivalences, with attention to bijections and cardinality.

**Graph theory.** The course explores graphs, trees, connectivity, paths, Euler and Hamiltonian cycles, and graph representations, connecting theory to real-world networks and algorithms.

**Combinatorics.** Students learn counting principles, permutations, combinations, the pigeonhole principle, and recurrence relations, developing skills for complexity analysis and algorithm design.

**Boolean algebra and applications.** The course covers Boolean functions, minimization, and their application in digital circuits, linking discrete math to computer architecture.

**Outcome.** The course provides a rigorous foundation for algorithms, data structures, cryptography, and theoretical computer science, with hands-on practice in formal reasoning and problem solving.
