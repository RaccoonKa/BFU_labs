# Numerical Methods

An educational course covering the fundamental numerical methods used to solve mathematical problems with computers. The materials include lab assignments on approximation, interpolation, numerical integration, and solving differential equations.

## What the course is about

The course is built around practical implementation of numerical algorithms and understanding their accuracy, stability, and computational cost.

**Approximation and interpolation.** Students learn to approximate functions with polynomials, understand interpolation (Lagrange, Newton), and evaluate approximation error.

**Numerical integration and differentiation.** Lab assignments cover classical methods (rectangle, trapezoid, Simpson) and their convergence, as well as numerical differentiation and its pitfalls.

**Solving equations and systems.** The course explores root-finding algorithms (bisection, Newton, secant) and iterative methods for linear systems, with attention to convergence conditions.

**Differential equations.** Students implement Euler and Runge–Kutta methods for ordinary differential equations, comparing stability and accuracy on real examples.

**2D and 3D visualization.** A significant part of the course is dedicated to visualizing numerical results — from function plots to 3D surfaces — to build intuition about how methods behave.

**Outcome.** The course provides hands-on experience with numerical algorithms and the skills to choose the right method, estimate error, and visualize results.
